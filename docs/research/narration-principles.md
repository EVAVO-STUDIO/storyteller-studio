# Narration Craft Principles

The platform should study excellent narration as a craft without cloning any
particular performer.

Areas of study include:

- storytelling intention rather than sentence reading;
- narrative distance and vocal intimacy;
- clear but restrained character differentiation;
- breath, silence and phrase architecture;
- emotional progression across scenes and chapters;
- humour delivered through timing rather than exaggeration;
- preserving authorial rhythm;
- handling exposition without becoming mechanical;
- maintaining clarity through dense prose;
- directing subtext rather than applying simple emotion labels;
- matching vocal energy to dramatic importance;
- maintaining continuity across sessions and books;
- avoiding repetitive synthetic cadence;
- allowing quiet passages to remain quiet;
- using visual accompaniment to deepen atmosphere rather than literally
  illustrating every sentence.

Named narrators and storytellers may be documented as craft references only.
Their identity or recognisable voice must not become a cloning target.
